# Coach — the evidence library

Sources Coach cites (DEC-149). Built 2026-10-31 by a research agent. Every DOI was resolved in Europe PMC and checked
against its abstract (or the open full text for WHO 2020, PAR-Q+ and AASM). Planner re-resolved the six 2023–2026 DOIs.
A claim not here = "general knowledge, not yet sourced". Accepted by Emilio 2026-10-31 (DEC-151). OA = open access.
When a newer source replaces an older one, the older one is marked *superseded*.

## 1. Public-health baseline
- **Adults: 150–300 min/week moderate or 75–150 vigorous aerobic, plus strength work for all major muscle groups ≥ 2
  days/week.** — Bull et al. 2020, WHO guidelines, Br J Sports Med — https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2020-102955 — guideline, OA.
- **65+: the same, plus varied balance + strength activity ≥ 3 days/week to prevent falls** (all older adults since 2020). — same.

## 2. Weekly volume
- **Muscle and strength both rise with weekly sets, with diminishing returns (faster for strength). An indirect set
  (e.g. triceps in a bench press) counts best as 0.5.** — Pelland et al. 2026, Sports Med — https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w — meta-regression (67 studies). No plateau set count in the abstract: don't quote a "max sets".
- **Hypertrophy: ≥ 10 sets/week per muscle. Strength: 2–3 sets per exercise, ≥ 2 sessions/week.** — Currier et al. 2026,
  ACSM Position Stand, Med Sci Sports Exerc 58(4):851–872 — https://doi.org/10.1249/mss.0000000000003897 — guideline, OA. **Replaces ACSM 2009.**
- **Time-limited minimum: ≥ 4 sets/week per muscle at 6–15 RM, with a leg press/squat, an upper pull and an upper push.**
  — Iversen et al. 2021, *No Time to Lift?*, Sports Med — https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-021-01490-1 — narrative review, OA.
- **Maintenance: strength and size held up to 32 weeks on 1 session/week, 1 set per exercise, if the load stays; older
  adults may need 2 sessions and 2–3 sets.** — Spiering et al. 2021, J Strength Cond Res — https://doi.org/10.1519/jsc.0000000000003964 — narrative review.
- *Superseded/older:* Schoenfeld et al. 2017 (+0.37 % growth per weekly set) — https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2016.1210197; Ralston et al. 2017 (strength dose-response) — https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-017-0762-7.

## 3. Frequency per muscle
- **At equal volume, frequency doesn't meaningfully change hypertrophy — choose by preference.** — Schoenfeld et al. 2019,
  J Sports Sci — https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2018.1555906 — meta-analysis. Pelland 2026 agrees.
- **Frequency helps strength, with diminishing returns** (Pelland 2026), mostly via the extra volume it allows — Grgic et al.
  2018, Sports Med — https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-018-0872-x.
- *Superseded:* Schoenfeld et al. 2016 ("twice a week beats once") — https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-016-0543-8.

## 4. Effort: failure, RIR, RPE
- **Hypertrophy improves as sets end closer to failure; strength is similar across a wide RIR range.** — Robinson et al.
  2024, Sports Med — https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-024-02069-2 — meta-regression (RIR estimated).
- **Training to failure isn't needed** (trivial edge, ES 0.19). — Refalo et al. 2023, Sports Med — https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-022-01784-y — OA. ACSM 2026 agrees.
- **RIR-based RPE: RPE 10 = 0 reps left, 9 = 1, …** — Zourdos et al. 2016, J Strength Cond Res — https://doi.org/10.1519/jsc.0000000000001049.
- **People misjudge reps left; better close to failure, heavier loads (≤ 12 reps), later sets.** — Halperin et al. 2022,
  Sports Med — https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-021-01559-x.

## 5. Load and rep ranges
- **Hypertrophy is similar across loads when sets are hard; strength is best with heavy loads (≥ 80 % 1RM).** — Lopez et
  al. 2021, Med Sci Sports Exerc — https://doi.org/10.1249/mss.0000000000002585 — OA; Currier et al. 2023, Br J Sports Med — https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2023-106807 — OA (top: heavy, multi-set, 3×/week strength; 2×/week hypertrophy).

## 6. Rest between sets
- **More than 60 s gives a small hypertrophy benefit; no clear gain past ~90 s.** — Singer et al. 2024, Front Sports Act
  Living — https://doi.org/10.3389/fspor.2024.1429789 — OA.
- **Trained men: 3 min beat 1 min for 1RM and quad size over 8 weeks.** — Schoenfeld et al. 2016, J Strength Cond Res — https://doi.org/10.1519/jsc.0000000000001272 — RCT, n = 21.
- *Superseded:* ACSM 2009, 3–5 min heavy strength, 1–2 min at 6–12 RM — https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0b013e3181915670.

## 7. Progression
- **Add 2–10 % load when 1–2 reps above target on the current load; novices 8–12 RM, 2–3 days/week.** — ACSM 2009
  (above). The 2026 update says "progressive" without restating the rule — treat it as the older standard.
- **Adding reps at a fixed load ≈ adding load at fixed reps (8 weeks, trained).** — Plotkin et al. 2022, PeerJ — https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.14142 — RCT, OA. Supports double progression.
- **Periodization didn't consistently change outcomes.** — ACSM 2026.

## 8. Deloads (thin evidence)
- **Definition: a planned period of reduced training stress for recovery.** — Bell et al. 2023, Sports Med Open — https://doi.org/10.1186/s40798-023-00633-0 — expert consensus, OA.
- **A full week off mid-program cost some lower-body strength** (Coleman et al. 2024, PeerJ — https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.16777);
  **reduced volume and frequency in weeks 4 and 8 cost nothing** (Pancar et al. 2026, Sci Rep — https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-40612-5, untrained men).

## 9. Protein
- **Gains level off at ~1.6 g/kg/day.** — Morton et al. 2018, Br J Sports Med — https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2017-097608 — 49 RCTs, OA.
- **Small extra gains at ≥ 1.6 g/kg (under 65) and 1.2–1.59 g/kg (65+).** — Nunes et al. 2022, J Cachexia Sarcopenia Muscle — https://doi.org/10.1002/jcsm.12922 — OA.
- **1.4–2.0 g/kg/day for most exercisers; 20–40 g per meal every 3–4 h.** — Jäger et al. 2017, ISSN — https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-017-0177-8 — OA.
- **In a deficit, lean trained people: 2.3–3.1 g/kg of fat-free mass** (not body weight). — Helms et al. 2014, IJSNEM — https://doi.org/10.1123/ijsnem.2013-0054.

## 10. Fat loss
- **Lose ~0.5–1 % of bodyweight a week to keep muscle (lean, trained).** — Helms, Aragon, Fitschen 2014, JISSN — https://doi.org/10.1186/1550-2783-11-20 — OA.
  Garthe et al. 2011 (https://doi.org/10.1123/ijsnem.21.2.97): slower loss (0.7 %/wk) gained lean mass, faster didn't.
- **A sustained deficit drives fat loss; more body fat allows a larger deficit; adherence decides long-term success.** — Aragon et al. 2017, ISSN — https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-017-0174-y — OA.
- **A deficit blunts lean-mass gains (not strength); > ~500 kcal/day prevented them.** — Murphy & Koehler 2022, Scand J Med Sci Sports — https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.14075.
- **Strength training during calorie restriction kept ~93 % of the lean mass otherwise lost** (obese older adults). — Sardeli et al. 2018, Nutrients — https://doi.org/10.3390/nu10040423 — OA.
- **~7,000 steps/day: meaningful health gains** (health, not fat loss; observational). — Ding et al. 2025, Lancet Public Health — https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(25)00164-1; Paluch et al. 2022 — https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(21)00302-9.

## 11. Supersets
- **Supersets shorten sessions without losing volume or long-term gains; harder-feeling, more fatigue; agonist–antagonist
  pairs keep volume best.** — Zhang et al. 2025, Sports Med — https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-025-02176-8 — OA. Iversen 2021: roughly half the time.

## 12. Cardio + strength
- **Cardio doesn't hurt size or max strength; it blunts explosive strength, mostly in the same session — ≥ 3 h apart avoids it.** — Schumann et al. 2022, Sports Med — https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-021-01587-7 — OA.
- **Small lower-body strength interference in men, not women.** — Huiberts et al. 2024, Sports Med — https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-023-01943-9 — OA.

## 13. HIIT and Tabata
- **Original Tabata = 8 × 20 s all-out (~170 % VO2max) / 10 s on a bike, in trained young men.** A bodyweight "Tabata"
  isn't that stimulus. — Tabata et al. 1996, Med Sci Sports Exerc — https://doi.org/10.1097/00005768-199610000-00018.
- **HIIT ≈ continuous training for VO2max and body composition, in less time** (Milanović 2015 — https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-015-0365-0; Wewege 2017 — https://doi.org/10.1111/obr.12532).
  Viana 2019 found more fat loss with intervals (https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2018-099928); Sultana 2019 found low-volume HIIT doesn't change body fat (https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-019-01167-w).

## 14. Older adults
- **Exercise cuts falls by 23 %; mainly balance + functional work.** — Sherrington et al. 2019, Cochrane — https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD012424.pub2.
- **Power training (lifting fast) improves function a bit more than plain strength training** (low certainty). — Balachandran et al. 2022, JAMA Netw Open — https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2022.11623 — OA.
- **Supervised heavy + impact training raised spine bone density (postmenopausal, low bone mass).** — Watson et al. 2018, J Bone Miner Res — https://doi.org/10.1002/jbmr.3284.
- NSCA position (Fragala 2019 — https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000003230): its sets/%1RM numbers not verified.

## 15. Screening and pain
- **PAR-Q+ (2025 form): a yes → follow-up questions → ePARmed-X+ or a qualified professional.** — https://eparmedx.com/ ; Warburton et al. 2011 — https://doi.org/10.1139/h11-060.
- **ACSM screening: current activity, symptoms or known heart/metabolic/kidney disease, and the intended intensity.** — Riebe et al. 2015 — https://doi.org/10.1249/mss.0000000000000664.
- **Chronic pain: exercise allowing some pain had a small short-term edge over pain-free; "pain … need not be a barrier".** — Smith et al. 2017, Br J Sports Med — https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2016-097383 — OA. Chronic pain only; new or acute pain → a professional.

## 16. Sleep
- **Adults ≥ 7 h a night.** — Watson et al. 2015, AASM/SRS — https://doi.org/10.5665/sleep.4716.
- **Sleep loss cuts performance ~7.6 %.** — Craven et al. 2022, Sports Med — https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-022-01706-y — OA. One night without sleep cut muscle protein synthesis 18 % (Lamon 2021 — https://doi.org/10.14814/phy2.14660).

## 17. Adherence
- **What keeps people training:** supervision, enjoyment, fitting it into daily life, social support, feedback and progress
  tracking, self-efficacy, goals, an active role, realistic expectations. — Collado-Mateo et al. 2021, IJERPH — https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18042023 — umbrella review, OA (patients and older adults).
- **A habit takes a median ~2 months (4–335 days).** — Singh et al. 2024, Healthcare — https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare12232488 — OA, high risk of bias.

## Not verified — don't cite
Fragala 2019's numbers; Ralston 2017's set cut-offs; Morton 2018's upper limit (often quoted ~2.2 g/kg); Pelland 2026's
plateau; steps as a fat-loss tool; ACSM 2026's rest and progression specifics.

## Where the evidence disagrees
- **Frequency:** "twice a week" (2016) vs no effect at equal volume (2019, 2026); it still helps strength.
- **Failure:** closer is better for size (Robinson 2024) vs trivial (Refalo 2023, ACSM 2026). Train hard; failure optional.
- **Deloads:** a week off cost strength (Coleman 2024); reduced volume cost nothing (Pancar 2026). Thin.
- **HIIT for fat loss:** more (Viana 2019), equal (Wewege 2017), none at low volume (Sultana 2019). All agree: fitness, fast.
- **Rest:** 3 min (one RCT) vs no gain past ~90 s (Singer 2024).
- **Protein in a deficit:** ISSN's 2.3–3.1 g/kg is per kg **fat-free mass** in its source (Helms 2014). Use FFM.
